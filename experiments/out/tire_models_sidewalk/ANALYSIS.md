# Tire laws calibrated on kim quad + slope sidewalk, pooled

Generated by

```bash
python -m experiments.scooter_tire_models fit --model <law> --surfaces kim_quad slope_sidewalk             # five laws, two runs held out
python -m experiments.scooter_tire_models fit --model <law> --all-data --surfaces kim_quad slope_sidewalk  # five laws, all 20 runs
python -m experiments.scooter_tire_models profile --model <law> --surfaces kim_quad slope_sidewalk
python -m experiments.scooter_tire_models report --surfaces kim_quad slope_sidewalk
python -m experiments.scooter_tire_model_plots --surfaces kim_quad slope_sidewalk
```

Files here: `parameters.md` / `parameters.csv` (the parameter tables), `report.md` (every accuracy table),
`<law>.json` / `<law>.pt` (full results and weights), `<law>_profile.json`, `plots/`.

## Data and protocol

| | runs | moving | distance | lateral acceleration p95 / max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| concrete (kim quad) | 9 | 429 s | 714 m | 1.23 / 2.29 m/s² |
| sloped sidewalk | 11 | 514 s | 835 m | 1.15 / 2.43 m/s² |

`scooter_calibrate`'s protocol, unchanged: corrected logs, 1.5 s windows, vx pinned to the wheel speed, both
wheels free-rolling, IMU slope input, variance-normalized yaw-rate + heading loss, Adam (lr 0.01, 150 steps)
from the scooter preset, front and rear tire tied. Every law starts at the same 4,500 N/rad and frees the same
four things: its stiffness parameter, `yaw_inertia`, `yaw_damping`, residual steering zero. Friction and
curve-shape parameters are held. The last run of each folder is held out (163726 and 191928), leaving 547
training windows from 18 runs.

**Which fit the numbers come from.** Parameters are from the all-data fits (all 20 runs, 617 windows).
Accuracy is from the held-out fits (18 runs), scored on the two runs they never saw. The two sets of
parameters agree: stiffness within 1-5%, yaw inertia within 0.2%, damping within 4%.

## What the numbers say

1. **Calibration generalizes for every law.** Held-out loss falls 7-9% from the preset (for example brush
   15.65 -> 14.47) alongside a 3-5% fall on the training runs, and held-out GPS ADE falls from 0.49 m to
   0.28-0.30 m.

2. **The five laws are interchangeable on this data.** Fiala, Dugoff and Pacejka agree to 0.1% in held-out
   loss and to 1 mm in GPS ADE; the brush is 0.5% behind and Burckhardt 1.7% behind, and both gaps are
   stiffness, not curve shape (next point). The reason is in `plots/force_curves.png`: the largest lateral
   load any run puts on the front tire is about 103 N, 31% of `mu Fz` = 334 N, and the 95th percentile is
   51 N. Below that every law is a straight line through the origin, reached at under 0.6 deg of slip. The
   recordings never enter the region where the laws differ (peak, saturation, fall-off), so nothing here can
   rank them, and `mu`, the Pacejka shape factors and Burckhardt's `c1`, `c3` are not identifiable.

3. **Cornering stiffness is a lower bound, and the 11,000-26,000 N/rad spread is the optimizer, not the
   tire.** `plots/stiffness_profile.png`: loss drops steeply up to about 7,000 N/rad, then flattens; from
   10,000 to 30,000 N/rad it falls by only 0.5% (train) and 1.5% (held-out), and at equal stiffness the five
   laws agree within 0.4% from 15,000 N/rad up. All five were still climbing at step 150. How far each got depends on its
   coordinate: `cy` moves fastest, the brush's `cp` is log-scaled over eight decades and moves slower,
   Burckhardt's `c2` slowest. Read the result as "at least ~10,000 N/rad" (24 x Fz per radian). It is
   consistent with the single-surface brush fits in CALIBRATION.md (15,000-16,500 N/rad).

4. **Yaw inertia is pinned, damping loosely.** Inertia is 7.02-7.12 kg m² across the five laws (1.5% spread),
   the same to 0.2% between the held-out and all-data fits, and matches the 7.0-7.2 found per surface. Damping is 0.26-0.33 with the loss flat below ~1, so treat it as
   an upper bound. The residual steering zero is +0.02-0.05 deg motor, i.e. the loader's per-run zero is
   right. The steady-state yaw gain matches the measured one to 0.4% for every law (L_eff 0.950 m).

5. **Against the kinematic bicycle the result is mixed.** Over 1.5-3 s windows the bicycle is slightly better:
   5% lower yaw loss, 2% lower yaw-rate RMSE, 0.1 deg lower median heading error. At that horizon the
   residual is gyro noise and rider input, identical for every model (`plots/holdout.png`, middle row). Over
   whole runs the fitted tire models are better: held-out GPS ADE 0.28-0.30 m against 0.38 m (25% lower),
   Frechet 9% lower, and lower ADE on 16-17 of the 20 runs. The models' yaw inertia and tire compliance
   filter steering jitter that the bicycle integrates straight into heading.

6. **The gain is on the sloped sidewalk.** Concrete: 0.81 vs 0.83 m (2%). Sloped sidewalk: 0.44 vs 0.55 m
   (19%). Run 190606, the steepest grade, goes from 1.01 m to 0.49 m with the slope input. Two runs go the
   other way: 190610 (1.22 vs 1.02 m) and 163031 (2.35 vs 2.29 m); the latter is the foot-down pivot
   described in CALIBRATION.md that no rolling model represents and dominates the concrete average.

7. **Recommendation.** Use any of brush, Fiala, Dugoff or Pacejka with C_alpha around 15,000-25,000 N/rad,
   yaw_inertia 7.1 kg m², yaw_damping 0.3, mu held at 0.8. Keep the brush as the default since it is the
   model's native law and also supplies aligning moment. Burckhardt needs more optimizer steps (or a larger
   learning rate on `c2`) to reach the same stiffness; it is not a worse law. Separating the laws needs data
   with lateral acceleration above roughly 5 m/s², where the tire leaves the linear region.

## Caveats

- `fit time` in `report.md` is wall-clock and includes periods when the laptop slept; ignore it for brush,
  fiala and dugoff. A fit takes about 25-30 minutes of CPU and peaks near 5 GB of memory, so run at most
  three at once on a 24 GB machine.
- The brush curve overshoots `mu Fz` by about 5% near 5 deg of slip. That is the law's smooth
  adhesion/sliding blend at high stiffness, far outside the recorded range, and does not affect these fits.
- Short-window position errors use wheel-plus-gyro dead reckoning as the reference, which shares the wheel
  speed with every model; the GPS columns are the independent check.
